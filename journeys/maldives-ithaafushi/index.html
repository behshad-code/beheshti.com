JOURNEYS / MALDIVES

Seven Nights,
Never Enough.

Life above the lagoon at Waldorf Astoria Maldives Ithaafushi.

Explore the story ↓
The Maldives · a personal photo essay
THE WELL-LIVED LIFE / 01

A private overwater villa. Unhurried mornings. An island within an island. Meals worth remembering. And a sandbar with no one else in sight.

We came for seven nights. Somewhere between the first breakfast and the last sunset, we began to suspect it would not be enough.

01 An island, approached by sea

The arrival is part of the story.

There is a moment when the skyline disappears, and you realize that, for the next week, the ocean will set the pace.

Seven nights sounded generous when we planned this trip. After all, how much could there possibly be to do on a small island in the middle of the Indian Ocean?

We were there to celebrate our anniversary, and the journey to the island already felt like part of the celebration. The private-yacht transfer took about 45 minutes, with Champagne, caviar and snacks on board. Beyond the boat, the sea kept changing shades of blue until the villas of Waldorf Astoria Maldives Ithaafushi came into view.

More Champagne, welcome treats and a fruit basket were waiting when we arrived. The welcome was thoughtful, the atmosphere quiet, and the horizon seemed to have no intention of ending. We had come to slow down, eat exceptionally well, and occasionally do absolutely nothing.

Our first evening brought Lebanese and Middle Eastern food on the beach. It was a lovely beginning to the week: dinner beside the water, with the rest of the itinerary still ahead of us.

02 Overwater living

A villa with its own sense of time.

Our two-bedroom overwater villa made it remarkably easy to forget the rest of the world.

There are beautiful hotel rooms, and then there are rooms that fundamentally change how a day unfolds. Our two-bedroom overwater villa with a pool belonged firmly in the second category.

The terrace opened directly toward the sea. There was a swing, generous room for lounging, a private pool, and a bathtub positioned so that even the smallest moment felt connected to the horizon. After dark, the pool became a ribbon of turquoise light against the black ocean.

Each of us had a bicycle for getting around the resort, though returning to the villa never felt like going indoors. With so much space and water around us, it was easy to linger there longer than planned.

03 Breakfast and the slower hours

An entire morning devoted to the good things.

From freshly cooked noodles to charcuterie, tropical fruit and breakfast by the beach, the mornings quickly became a ritual.

Breakfast had the sort of range that makes choosing only one thing seem unreasonable. Fresh greens and fruit, cheeses and cured meats, grains and freshly prepared dishes, plus a chef working at the noodle station. The details mattered as much as the variety.

And yet the best part of breakfast was not a particular dish. It was the setting. Sitting near the sand, looking across the lagoon, we had no urgency to move on to the next appointment. We could simply enjoy it.

04 An island to ourselves, almost

Pool, beach, wildlife and the art of not checking the time.

There were stretches when it seemed that the entire resort had been left for us to enjoy.

The beach was breathtaking, the pool inviting, and the atmosphere almost improbably private. No crowds to navigate. No noise beyond the breeze, the water and the occasional conversation. We spent our days drifting between the pool and beach, never quite feeling the need to choose between them.

Lunch could be as simple as a fresh ahi poke bowl by the water. A cocktail, a plate of tropical fruit, or an afternoon spent looking out at the lagoon was more than enough.

And the island was very much alive. Tropical fish in the water, little crabs near the sand, and fruit bats tucked among the palms. It felt natural and happy, which is a better description of paradise than most travel brochures manage.

05 An island within the island

The rare privilege of seeing Ithaafushi Private Island.

We visited the resort’s extraordinary private-island retreat, toured the facilities, and enjoyed some time on its tennis and padel courts.

Even within the extraordinary setting of Ithaafushi, there is another level of privacy. The separately accessible Ithaafushi Private Island is designed for exclusive use, with accommodations for up to 18 guests.

We arrived by boat from the main resort and had the chance to tour the facilities. The scale of the residences, the open lounge, the pool and the secluded setting were exceptional. We even made time for some tennis and padel. It was a visit rather than an overnight stay, but quite an introduction to how far the idea of a private island can go.

06 A table under the stars

The most convenient reservation on the island.

One evening, we decided the best restaurant might be our own terrace.

We had dinner arranged at the villa beside the pool. The table was set, the water was lit, and the ocean beyond was almost invisible against the night sky. The menu brought together Asian, Indian and Maldivian flavors: crisp starters, vibrant salads, aromatic rice, rich curries and beautifully cooked prawns.

We could have spent the evening in one of the resort’s restaurants, of course. But an unhurried dinner under the stars, with no journey home afterward, felt like the only proper choice.

07 The duck that we still talk about

Li Long · A House of Shanghai Heritage

One of our favorite evenings was spent at Li Long, where the overwater setting was almost as memorable as the kitchen.

Li Long draws on the atmosphere of Shanghai’s historic residences, but the real reason to come is the food. We sat outside at the water’s edge, with the lagoon extending into the evening, and ordered the signature tasting menu.

Then came the roasted Beijing duck: lacquered skin, tender slices, thin Chinese pancakes, cucumber, scallions and hoisin sauce. The duck is prepared in what the resort describes as the Maldives’ first classic wood-fired oven. For us, it was the standout dish of the evening.

The pleasure was in the whole ritual: taking a little duck, adding the accompaniments, rolling the pancake and immediately wanting another.

The wood-fired preparation and dining concept are described on Li Long’s official page.

08 Japanese dining above the lagoon

Zuma Maldives · Contemporary izakaya

Another evening, another reason to reconsider leaving.

Zuma has a recognizable rhythm: an open kitchen, ingredients handled with confidence, plates arriving to be shared and an atmosphere that is sophisticated without being stiff. In the Maldives, it all happens above the water.

We sat by the large window, watching the last light fade beyond the overwater villas. The table filled gradually with sushi, grilled seafood and beautifully balanced small dishes. There were crisp, spicy bites, rich flavors from the grill and more than one reason to raise a glass.

It is easy for a famous restaurant name to feel imported. Here, the view and the kitchen worked together so naturally that the setting became part of the meal.

09 Pizza and a cold beer

A little perspective.

After all the tasting menus and extraordinary restaurants, one of the simplest combinations was still hard to beat.

There is a place for elaborate dinners and there is a place for pizza and beer by the pool. Ideally, both places are in the Maldives.

A crisp, wood-fired pizza, an ice-cold beer, and turquoise water stretching in every direction. Nothing to explain, nothing to improve. It may have been the least complicated meal of the trip and one of the most satisfying.

10 The land that disappears

A morning on the sandbars.

Some of the most remarkable places in the Maldives are not really islands at all.

We took jet skis out across the lagoon, leaving the resort behind and heading for a bright ribbon of white sand in the middle of the water. When we arrived, there was nobody else in sight. For that morning, it seemed like our own tiny corner of the Indian Ocean.

Sandbars are temporary and shifting features shaped by waves, currents and tides. They can change shape, become smaller, or slip beneath the water as conditions change. Standing on one, with water extending in every direction, gives you a very different sense of what an island is.

There is also an unexpected connection to life on the reef. Parrotfish graze on coral reef surfaces and help generate the sand-sized carbonate sediment that contributes to Maldivian reef islands. It is an elegant reminder that the landscape is not static, and that small creatures can help shape something enormous.

The ride back was exhilarating. The quiet of the sandbar was even better.

THE NATURAL STORY

How reef life helps build islands

Peer-reviewed research on Maldivian reefs documents the contribution of parrotfish to coral-derived sediment. Waves and currents then redistribute this material into beaches, sandbars and island deposits. Not every sandbar vanishes at each high tide, and their size and persistence vary with local conditions.

11 The hour that stayed with us

Champagne, a little caviar, and the view.

Some places have sunsets. The Maldives gives the entire evening a different pace.

By late afternoon, the colors softened and the lagoon seemed to change its character every few minutes. A glass of Champagne was often close at hand. So, rather happily, was caviar. After that, perhaps a cocktail before dinner.

There is a certain humor in how effortlessly one gets used to such arrangements. Yet the part we remember most is quieter: the light on the water, the way the sky changes as the sun drops, and that sense of having nowhere else to be.

12 Until we meet again, Maldives

The only difficult part of seven nights.

Goodbyes are easier when you already have a return trip in mind.

After seven days and seven nights, the one thing we weren’t looking forward to was leaving.

Our butler and the wonderful Waldorf Astoria crew gathered at the jetty to see us off. As our boat pulled away, everyone waved goodbye. They seemed genuinely sad to see us go, and honestly, the feeling was mutual.

It is remarkable how quickly a place can become familiar, especially when you have spent a week surrounded by beautiful beaches, turquoise lagoons, wonderful food and people who make every day feel special.

The boat ride back to the airport gave us one final chance to admire the Maldives from the water. Gradually, the little islands and endless blue horizons gave way to the skyline of Malé. Reality was slowly making its way back into the picture.

At least we were heading home completely relaxed, refreshed and ready for our fast-tracked airport experience and flight back to Dubai.

We came for seven nights thinking that would be plenty of time. We left wondering how another seven nights could possibly hurt.

So long, Maldives. We’ll be back in a few years. And next time, perhaps we’ll stay a little longer.
